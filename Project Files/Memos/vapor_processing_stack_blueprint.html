Phase 1: Bi-Directional Isolation (The Steam Cycle)

During the dewatering phase, the bottom port remains sealed. The primary induction coil drives energy into the shell, but the mass temperature is locked at the boiling point due to the latent heat of vaporization. The top solenoid manages the 15 PSI threshold, eventually triggering the steam explosion. Because pyrolysis hasn't begun, the steam exiting the top lid is largely free of heavy, sticky tars, preserving the integrity of the Viton O-rings and top-mounted solenoid seals.

Phase 2: Downdraft Autofiltration

Once water is evacuated, the top valve is locked shut. As the primary susceptor climbs to 300°C–400°C, VOCs, suspended particles, and heavy oils vaporize. Expanding gases create internal pressure. With the top closed, these gases are forced downward through the newly formed biochar. The porous biochar acts as a high-surface-area activated carbon filter, mechanically trapping heavy suspended particles before they can reach any plumbing.

Phase 3: The SiC Catalytic Choke Point

The bottom port contains a secondary induction zone. Because it utilizes a material with a much higher Curie temperature (e.g., a Silicon Carbide matrix or specialized ferrite alloy), the secondary coil drives this specific plug to 800°C - 1000°C.

  • Material: A porous SiC foam coated with transition metals (Nickel/Iron) to promote catalytic tar cracking.
  • Chemistry: As the heavy tars and VOCs are forced through this glowing hot, catalyzed matrix, they are thermally and chemically cracked into non-condensable syngas (CO, H2, CH4).
  • Result: The gas exiting the bottom port is a clean, dry syngas that will not foul downstream plumbing, ready to be routed to your PEM/NaOH scrubber cell.
